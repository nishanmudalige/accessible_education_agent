Introduction

To the teacher and the student

This French e-book is aimed at a first-year college student. Its features include:

1. Each chapter is built around communicative strategies. Clearly defined objectives in communication, culture, and grammar are given at the start of each chapter, and summary exercises at the end allow students to measure their mastery of these objectives.

2. The exercises in the in-class (A) sections are composed mainly of guided practice and extension activities, along with occasional comprehension checks and comprehensible input. The teacher can provide teacher-directed “setting-the-stage” activities, comprehension checks, and further comprehensible input before beginning each section. Many models are provided to the students to give them a secure context in which to practice their vocabulary before they are asked to produce independent language.

3. The grammar included is explained in a more narrative form and in more detail than is typical for first-year textbooks. The grammar (B) sections should be read by the students outside of class before the communicative activities requiring those grammar points are done in class. By providing more explicit grammatical detail than is usual in a first-year book, the authors hope to stimulate students to reflect on the grammar of their own language as well as of French, helping students to become aware that their study of French is not just about mastery of a new language and culture, but about a more critical view of their own.

4. The amount of grammar is less than is typically contained in a first-year text. The grammar included has been chosen to meet the needs of the communicative goals of each chapter. The grammatical concepts included in this book focus on those that will be needed for the sentences and questions that a typical low-intermediate speaker can form, and those are emphasized repeatedly.

5. The e-book implicitly and explicitly recycles material from previous chapters on a regular basis, so that students can see their learning as a continual progression rather than as a rush from one grammar point to the next.

6. The e-book is ideally used in a classroom with internet and projection capabilities; it contains hyperlinks to video and audio-based activities as well as navigational links to referenced exercises within the text itself.

7. Supplementary activities (“Activités Supplémentaires”) that accompany each chapter have been created to expand students’ knowledge of French culture in Canada and around the world. These additional activities are enriched with videos as well as French-Canadian music.

8. Plenty of interactive exercises (“Exercices interactifs”) can be found in this online edition to help students practice vocabulary and grammatical concepts taught in each chapter. The exercises follow the order in which the material is presented in each chapter.
